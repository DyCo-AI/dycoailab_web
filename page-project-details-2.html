Research / Application Research

Off-Road Autonomy and Intelligent Robotic Systems

Data-driven dynamics, adaptive control, and safe navigation beyond structured roads.

We connect Koopman and Perron–Frobenius operator methods with planning and feedback control for autonomous robots. Our work spans terrain-dependent vehicle modeling, predictive control, density-based safety, and legged locomotion, with validation in simulation and on robotic hardware.

01 / Data to dynamics

Data-Driven Vehicle Dynamics and Koopman Modeling

Off-road vehicles encounter changing traction, terrain geometry, and wheel–ground interactions. We develop situationally aware models that learn these effects from trajectory data and retain a structure suitable for prediction and control.

Koopman representations lift measured vehicle states into observable coordinates. A family of parameterized models captures different operating conditions, while auxiliary inputs account for environmental perturbations. Simulation and hardware data connect model identification with motion planning and feedback control.

Project Varuna brings these components together in an end-to-end workflow. Experimental work includes F1TENTH and the 1:5-scale Hunter SE platform; the broader research program investigates transfer to larger ground vehicles.

02 / Models in the loop

Adaptive and Predictive Control for Off-Road Vehicles

A single global vehicle model can lose accuracy as maneuvers and operating conditions change. The multi-model parameterized Koopman (MMPK) framework learns curvature-dependent predictors and uses them within local trajectory planning and model predictive control.

The IJRR work combines offline model learning with online planning and linear MPC for switched Koopman dynamics. Hunter SE experiments evaluate fishhook, skidpad, and slalom tracking. These benchmark maneuvers test model and controller behavior; they are distinct from the terrain-adaptation studies in Project Varuna.

Related research explores ensemble model selection and meta-adaptive Koopman operators, allowing the predictive model to respond to new vehicle behavior and operating regimes using limited new data.

03 / Safety through density

Safe Navigation and Motion Planning Using Control Density Functions

Density functions describe how trajectories occupy state space. This viewpoint supports analytical constructions that guide motion toward a target while avoiding unsafe regions, under the assumptions of the corresponding safety and convergence results.

Control density functions turn this structure into controller-synthesis constraints. Our work develops direct feedback laws and quadratic-program safety filters, and extends density-based constraints to predictive control and time-varying environments.

The accompanying code explores single-robot obstacle avoidance, multi-agent collision avoidance, local sensing, and moving obstacles. The examples shown here are numerical simulations from the research repository, with outcomes tied to each model, controller, and scenario.

04 / Learning for legged autonomy

Quadruped Locomotion and Legged Robot Autonomy

Residual Koopman MPC (RK-MPC) augments nominal single-rigid-body dynamics with a compact, learned residual model. The residual predictor operates in lifted coordinates and corrects model mismatch associated with contact variation and terrain disturbances.

The corrected predictor is embedded in a convex quadratic-program MPC formulation. The preprint reports onboard control at 500 Hz, with evaluation in Gazebo and on a Unitree Go1 across grass, gravel, snow, and ice, as well as debris and push-recovery tests.

These experiments study locomotion using proprioception and commanded motion references. They complement density-based navigation, which addresses where the robot should move and how it should avoid obstacles.
