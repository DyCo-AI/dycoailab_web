Research Overview

Nonlinear trajectories can be studied through linear evolution in spaces of functions and measures. At DyCoAI, we use this operator perspective to connect dynamical-systems theory with computational methods for stability, feedback design, optimal control, and safety.

Our contributions include Lyapunov measures for almost-everywhere stability, convex control formulations in the space of densities, and spectral Koopman methods for approximating nonlinear control problems. A central goal is to connect these mathematical constructions with finite-dimensional, data-driven computations while accounting for approximation error and the information available in finite data.

Koopman operator
Evolves observables: functions of the system state. Its spectral structure provides coordinates for studying nonlinear dynamics and their geometry.
Perron–Frobenius operator
Transports measures or densities of ensembles of states. Its duality with the Koopman operator connects function-based and measure-based analysis.

Research Directions

01 / Operator foundations

Linear Operator Perspectives on Nonlinear Dynamics

We move from individual nonlinear trajectories to the linear evolution of observables and distributions. The Koopman and Perron–Frobenius operators describe complementary aspects of the same dynamics, generally in infinite-dimensional spaces.

This dual perspective underlies our work on deterministic and stochastic stability. Finite-dimensional approximations make these ideas computationally useful, but their conclusions must be interpreted within the assumptions and resolution of the approximation.

02 / Stability and synthesis

Stability Analysis and Convex Optimal Control

Our published work introduces Lyapunov measures as a dual counterpart to Lyapunov functions for verifying almost-everywhere stability of attractor sets. Set-oriented approximations lead to linear-programming-based methods for computing these measures and designing stabilizing feedback.

We extend this perspective to convex density-based formulations of nonlinear optimal control, including stochastic problems with safety constraints. Data-driven approximations of transfer operators connect the infinite-dimensional formulation to finite-dimensional optimization.

03 / Spectrum and computation

Spectral Koopman Methods and Data-Driven Modeling

Koopman eigenfunctions encode dynamical information beyond a local state-space linearization. Under appropriate spectral and regularity assumptions, their level sets characterize invariant geometry, including stable and unstable manifolds.

Our published path-integral construction computes principal eigenfunctions from trajectory integrals and supports neural-network approximation. We also investigate reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) methods and approaches inspired by statistical mechanics, with an emphasis on useful approximations for analysis and feedback design.

04 / Value functions and safety

Hamilton–Jacobi Theory, Reachability, and Safety

Our published Koopman–Hamilton–Jacobi connection relates spectral analysis to Lagrangian submanifolds and the approximation of Hamilton–Jacobi solutions. This provides a route from operator eigenfunctions to value functions and nonlinear optimal control.

The spectral Koopman–Hopf work, listed as accepted for CDC 2026, extends this direction to adversarial reachability. It uses projected control and disturbance directions and Hamiltonian bounds to approximate value functions and backward reachable sets; these are approximation results under stated assumptions, not unrestricted safety guarantees.

Ongoing research / Under review

From Data to Certificates

The D2C preprint investigates Koopman supereigenfunctions: inequality-based growth envelopes intended to certify stability, safety, and uncertainty propagation directly from trajectory data. This work is under review and is distinct from the published results above. Continuing research also addresses finite-data accuracy and scalable approximations for nonlinear control.
